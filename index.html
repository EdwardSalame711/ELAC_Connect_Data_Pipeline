Client Overview

East Los Angeles College · Reading & Tutoring Center

01

Client Background

The project engagement centers on the reading and tutoring operations at East Los Angeles College (ELAC), supporting key administrative staff and lab directors like Maria Acosta.

02

Core Services & Operations

The reading center provides both in-person and virtual academic support sessions, utilizing tools like online chat systems (TalkTo) to connect students with tutors.

03

Data & Tracking Workflow

The center relies on attendance and scheduling platforms—historically transitioning through systems like ACE, Pisces, and currently Penji—to track student visits and compile positive attendance reports for institutional deans.

04

Current Challenges & Bottlenecks

Due to hardware limitations—such as barcode scanners failing to consistently read student IDs or ink quality issues on cards—the center maintains a hybrid workflow using manual paper sign-in logs to verify attendance data accuracy. Staff members spend dedicated weekly hours reviewing, filtering, and cleaning raw data entries to ensure compliance and correct reporting.

Project Goal and Scope

Define the project goal, boundaries, outcomes, and constraints

Project Goal

Our project will help Monte Perez at East Los Angeles College capture positive attendance as accurately as possible across learning support services—both those actively collecting and reporting data and those not yet reporting. We will know we have succeeded when we develop an easy-to-use solution that identifies missing information and can also serve our sister colleges and other campuses using Penji.

Project Overview

The project addresses the inefficiencies and manual reporting hurdles faced by campus tutoring and reading centers when tracking student attendance. Staff currently rely on a combination of Penji, legacy software, and manual paper sign-in logs because of hardware issues such as barcode scanner failures. The goal is to streamline attendance capture, data cleaning, and positive-attendance reporting for administrative stakeholders.

Objectives

  • Eliminate dependency on error-prone paper logs for physical student check-ins.
  • Reduce weekly administrative hours spent manually cross-referencing, formatting, and filtering invalid student ID data.
  • Create seamless and accurate end-of-semester positive-attendance reporting for institutional deans and administrators.

Deliverables

  • A comprehensive user-experience and workflow analysis of current campus tutoring-center data processes.
  • Wireframes or system-requirement specifications for an optimized attendance-tracking and reporting workflow.
  • Documentation and meeting logs detailing client needs from stakeholders such as Nelly and Maria.

In Scope

  • Analyze front-desk check-in processes for both in-person and virtual online-chat sessions.
  • Address data-entry error management, including duplicate digits, invalid IDs, and incorrect timestamps.
  • Evaluate compliance requirements such as ADA-compliant communication tools.

Out of Scope

  • Replace district-wide hardware infrastructure, including purchasing expensive RFID student ID cards or new campus-wide scanners.
  • Manage back-end institutional database architecture outside the reading center's direct reporting tools.

Constraints & Assumptions

Constraints

  • Limited budget for expensive hardware solutions.
  • Reliance on existing software ecosystems such as Penji.
  • Adherence to accessibility deadlines and ADA-compliance guidelines.

Assumptions

  • Staff will continue to require verification safeguards to ensure accurate positive-attendance data despite hardware limitations.

Data Quality

An instructional aide responsible for manually checking, cross-referencing, and cleaning raw data from Penji to ensure accurate positive-attendance reporting.

Role / Context: Instructional Aide at the East Los Angeles College Language Lab.

Goals
  • Produce an accurate, easy-to-read positive-attendance output that clearly presents each student ID number and session duration.
  • Use a system that automatically highlights incomplete data points for review.
Frustrations
  • Rows are often duplicated or left blank.
  • Rows frequently have incomplete information, requiring cross-referencing with other systems to resolve them or potentially deleting them.
  • Incomplete or inaccurate entries must be found manually, and the current visible output makes that process slow.

  • “I have to go through each of these individually before I can download the CSV, and I see that it’s not entered, so I will have to do it manually.”
What Success Looks Like
  • A system that reduces the manual labor and time required to cross-reference, sort, and correct inaccurate Penji data before it is reported to OIEA. This would remove recurring frustrations and save hours of work each reporting cycle.

Maria Acosta

Writing Center Director

Center Operations

The director responsible for making the data-correction process as efficient and accurate as possible before reporting and final publication.

Role / Context: Director of the East Los Angeles College Writing Center.

Goals
  • Create a system with safeguards that removes incorrect IDs—such as phone numbers, student ID numbers, and email addresses—and corrects inaccurate hours.

Process Flow

Penji raw-data preparation and distribution workflow

INFODiagram Information and Steps 1–18

Diagram Information

This workflow shows how staff validate and clean Penji utilization data, reconcile records against supporting sources, prepare the final file, and email it to Vanessa. Vanessa dispatches the file to A&R for enrollment blocking and to the Dean Assistant for the Dean’s Office; the process ends after both recipients receive the file.

Steps 1–18

  1. Begin the process.
  2. Open the required Penji tabs and supporting records.
  3. Verify walk-ins and appointments against paper tutor logs, rosters, and Cranium Cafe or ConnexEd login records.
  4. Correct out-of-bounds metrics, including invalid or inappropriate Zoom links.
  5. Download the unvetted CSV file from Penji.
  6. Scrub the CSV in Excel.
    • 6.1. Verify IDs.
    • 6.2. Remove duplicate entries.
    • 6.3. Remove staff and test entries.
    • 6.4. Identify and correct inverted timestamps.
    • 6.5. Review and correct extreme hour values.
  7. Look up missing information in rosters using the individual’s email address or name.
  8. Determine whether each row is resolvable and valid.
  9. If a row is not resolvable or valid, delete it from the Excel file.
  10. If a row is resolvable and valid, simplify the Math and Language Lab columns and calculate the summed decimal hours in Excel.
  11. Compare the calculated totals with the Penji Dashboard Utilization graph.
  12. Prepare the final file.
  13. Send the final file to Vanessa by email.
  14. Vanessa receives the final file.
  15. Vanessa dispatches the file to both destinations.
    • 15.1. Send the file to A&R for enrollment blocking.
    • 15.2. Send the file to the Dean Assistant for the Dean’s Office.
  16. Confirm that A&R has received the file.
  17. Confirm that the Dean Assistant has received the file.
  18. End the process.
